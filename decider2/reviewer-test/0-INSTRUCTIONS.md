# Reviewer test — instructions

**~20 minutes. No code will be shown to you, and you should not go looking for
any.** If you find yourself wanting to open a `.py` file, stop and write down what
you wanted from it instead — that is the single most useful thing this exercise
can produce.

## What this is testing

Not you. **The artefact.** The design claims a non-programmer can verify a rule
against policy by reading a generated sheet. Nine of eleven independent attempts
at building such a sheet produced nothing at all, and the one good sheet that did
get built turned out to contradict its own decision record. So the claim is
untested, and it is the highest-ranked risk in the whole design.

If you cannot complete a task, **that is the result**. Please don't work harder
than a real reviewer would to rescue it.

## Read these three files, in order

1. `1-POLICY-EXTRACT.md` — the policy, as the Credit Policy Committee wrote it
2. `2-RULE-SHEET-TERM-0042.md` — what decider2 would generate for one rule
3. `3-DECISION-RECORD.md` — one real decision the system made

## The three tasks

### Task A — does the rule implement the policy?

Read the policy §7.4, then the rule sheet. **Does the rule do what the policy
says?** If anything is missing, different, or you cannot tell, say so and say
which part of the sheet left you unsure.

### Task B — was this decision correct?

Read the decision record. **Given the policy and the rule sheet, was this
application handled correctly?** Show how you reached your answer.

This is the task that failed last time. A reviewer's real job is not reading a
rule — it is reconciling a rule against something that actually happened.

### Task C — a policy change

The Committee changes §7.4.2's cap from 48 months to 36.

**What would you need to see to confirm that change was made correctly, and made
nowhere else?** You do not have to find it — describe what you would ask for.

---

## What I need back from you

Short answers are fine. For each task:

- **Your answer**, and roughly how confident you are
- **Where you had to guess**, or re-read, or wanted something that wasn't there
- **Any question a real reviewer would ask that these documents don't answer**

That last one is the most valuable. You know what reviewers actually challenge;
I am guessing.

## One thing you should know going in

**These documents may or may not contain a discrepancy.** I am not telling you
whether they do, because a real reviewer is never told either — determining that
*is* the job. If you find something, say what and how you found it. If you find
nothing, say that plainly; "I read all three and everything reconciled" is a
perfectly good answer and would itself be a finding.
